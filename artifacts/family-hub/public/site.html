The kitchen iPad

Hang it where everyone walks past

Put an iPad on the kitchen wall and it becomes the family's board: today's plans, whose turn it is, what's for dinner — seen on the way out the door, not buried in one person's phone.

  • Everyone sees the same day — kids check their own chores without asking.
  • Stays up to date on its own, and a screensaver with the time takes over when nobody's using it.
  • Hide it in one tap behind a picture when guests come over.
The calendar

One calendar, colour-coded by person

Connect the calendars you already use. Everyone's events land in one view, each in their own colour, on every device in the house.

  • Google and Outlook, both ways. Add an event here and it shows up there.
  • Who's driving. The question that actually causes the argument, answered on the event itself.
  • Choose what syncs. Your work calendar doesn't have to be everyone's business.
Chores & stars

Stop being the one who has to ask

Everyone can see their own list. Finishing it earns stars, and stars turn into the things they actually want — screen time, a payout, whatever you decide.

  • Set it once. Chores repeat on the days you choose, for the people you choose.
  • Rewards you set the price of. Or cash out stars for real money at your rate.
  • A parent PIN on anything you'd rather a nine-year-old couldn't change.
Meals & groceries

Answer "what's for dinner" once a week

Plan the week, and the grocery list builds itself from what you planned — sorted by aisle, so you're not doubling back for the milk.

  • Snap a recipe. Photograph a cookbook page or a card in your grandmother's handwriting, and it's saved.
  • Paste a link. Most recipe sites come straight in, ingredients and all.
  • 200+ ideas built in for the nights when nobody can think of anything to cook.
Celebrations

Remembered early enough to do something about it

Most reminders tell you about a birthday the morning of, which is the one moment you can't act on it. SuperHub works backwards from the date instead.

  • Thirty days out, while there's still time to order something or book the table.
  • A week before, the day before, the day itself. Check it off early and it still comes back when it matters.
  • Everyone gets the reminder, so remembering isn't one person's job any more.
